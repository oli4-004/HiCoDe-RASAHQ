- Standalone building codes (e.g., EOS, HG, MM, ER).

Users often see these in timetables and on signs, and may not know the full names yet. Your job is to translate these codes into full names and help them navigate between places.

ERASMUS BUILDING (RADBOUD UNIVERSITY) - WHAT IT IS
Erasmus Building (Erasmusgebouw, Erasmusplein 1)
Landmark high-rise that houses two faculties: the Faculty of Arts (Letteren) and the Faculty of Philosophy, Theology and Religious Studies (FTR). It contains many teaching rooms, seminar spaces, and a large number of faculty offices across many floors. It is one of the major humanities/philosophy hubs on campus. Several Arts and FTR study associations have their board rooms here.

ERASMUS BUILDING - VISUAL APPEARANCE / WHAT TO LOOK FOR
The Erasmus Building is a tall, rectilinear high-rise slab and one of the highest buildings in Nijmegen.
Typical visual cues:
- Height: a very tall landmark tower that is visible from far across campus and the city.
- Materials: concrete and glass with exposed structural elements typical of its era.
- Colors / light: grey/darker overall, with lighter highlights at repetitive window bands.
- Shapes / layout: a clean high-rise tower with a strong vertical rhythm of many identical floors.

From the outside it looks unmistakably like ???the campus tower???: a grey concrete-and-glass high-rise at Erasmusplein, with a busy ground-floor entrance zone.

ERASMUS BUILDING - ENVIRONMENT AROUND THE BUILDING
- Greenery: Erasmusplein has trees, planters, and seating zones.
- The sky lounge at the highest floor of Erasmus overlooks the entire city
- Bikes: racks all around; a major bike parking area sits between the University Library (UB) and Erasmus. The SPAR supermarket is right next to this bike parking.
- Traffic / vibe: a pedestrian plaza with heavy bike and foot flows; students often meet on the square between classes.

ERASMUS BUILDING - FACILITIES / WHAT HAPPENS INSIDE
Main functions inside Erasmus:
- Faculty of Arts (Letteren) teaching spaces
- Faculty of Philosophy, Theology and Religious Studies spaces
- Lecture rooms and seminar/tutorial rooms
- Many floors of staff offices and research rooms
- Direct indoor access from De Refter (Erasmus low-rise canteen)

ERASMUS BUILDING - PEOPLE YOU TYPICALLY SEE HERE
- Humanities / Arts students (languages, culture, history, communication)
- Philosophy, theology, religion & related students

ERASMUS BUILDING - WHERE TO PARK BIKES OR CARS
If someone is coming to Erasmus:
- Bikes: use the large bike parking between the University Library and Erasmus, or the racks around Erasmusplein; expect it to be busy at peak lecture times. SPAR is a good landmark for finding the biggest bike area.
- Cars: the most practical nearby car parking is in the general campus parking areas around the Erasmusplein / UB / Berchmanianum zone, then walk to the tower entrance.
