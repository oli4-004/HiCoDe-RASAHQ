UNIVERSITY BUSINESS CENTRE (UBC) (RADBOUD UNIVERSITY) - WHAT IT IS
University Business Centre (Universitair Bedrijvencentrum, Toernooiveld 100-154) is an administrative/business and service building that supports university operations and university-industry partnering. It is part of the Mercator / Science Park area and is largely leased to external, knowledge-intensive companies that collaborate with Radboud.

This is not a student-facing teaching hub: you come here for business/start-up offices, meetings, project work with external partners, or specific support services. No study associations are based here, and most students only enter if they have an appointment, internship, or collaboration in the Mercator/Science Park network.

UNIVERSITY BUSINESS CENTRE (UBC) - VISUAL APPEARANCE / WHAT TO LOOK FOR
UBC looks more like a business/office complex than a faculty building.
Typical visual cues:
- Scale and layout: a low-to-mid rise office block with multiple sections/entrances (because it covers a long address range: 100-154).
- Materials: practical office-style facades, a mix of glass and panel/brick bands typical of science-park business buildings.
- Overall vibe: quiet, professional, "workplace" atmosphere rather than a student crowd or lecture-hall feel.
- Signage: you may see "UBC," "Mercator," or company names/logos near entrances.

If you're walking along Toernooiveld and suddenly the buildings feel more "startup/industry/office" than "teaching/research," you're in the UBC zone.

UNIVERSITY BUSINESS CENTRE (UBC) - ENVIRONMENT AROUND THE BUILDING
- Zone: UBC sits in the Science Park / Mercator corner of campus on Toernooiveld.
- Greenery: wide Toernooiveld paths with grass and trees; more open and less plaza-like than central campus.
- Bikes: standard bike racks near entrances, but not the huge student bike seas you see at Erasmusplein/Library.
- Traffic / vibe: steady staff and visitor flow, light student flow. Peak moments are tied to workday rhythms more than lecture changes.

UNIVERSITY BUSINESS CENTRE (UBC) - PUBLIC TRANSPORT / HOW TO GET THERE
Nearest bus stop:
- Huygens Building (Huygensgebouw) is the official nearest bus stop for UBC.

From Nijmegen Central Station (bus):
- Take the campus corridor buses (commonly line 10 Heyendaal Shuttle and line 12 depending on route).
- Get off at Huygensgebouw.
- Walk into Toernooiveld toward the Mercator/Science Park side; UBC is a short walk deeper into the Toernooiveld business cluster.

From Nijmegen Heyendaal Station (train):
- Heyendaal station is within walking distance; follow the Toernooiveld direction toward Mercator/Science Park.

Tip:
If you're going there the first time, navigate to "Toernooiveld 100" and then follow UBC/Mercator signage for the right entrance.

UNIVERSITY BUSINESS CENTRE (UBC) - WHERE TO PARK BIKES OR CARS
Bike:
- Park at the racks near the relevant UBC entrance. Because the building spans multiple numbers, park by the entrance closest to your tenant/appointment.

Car:
- The practical nearby parking location is P9 Toernooiveld, right by the UBC/Toernooiveld zone.
- Paid parking applies on weekdays 07:00-18:00; leaving within 30 minutes is usually free if you exit promptly after paying at the machine.

UNIVERSITY BUSINESS CENTRE (UBC) - ACCESSIBILITY (IMPORTANT FOR ROUTING)
- Wheelchair/scooter: after entering, the building is mostly independently accessible.
- Important detail: the front door is not fully independently accessible; call reception if you need assistance.
- Reception desk is present.
- Accessible toilet: ground floor.

QUICK "FACTS" / USEFUL CONTEXT
- UBC is basically the "business/startup gateway" of Radboud's Science Park: it's where university knowledge and external companies meet.
- Don't expect lecture halls or student services here; it's an office/partnering building first.
- The long address range (Toernooiveld 100-154) means multiple entrances - always check which number your appointment is tied to.
- If your bus stops at Huygensgebouw, you're close, but still need to walk into the Mercator/Toernooiveld corner rather than staying at the central campus paths.
