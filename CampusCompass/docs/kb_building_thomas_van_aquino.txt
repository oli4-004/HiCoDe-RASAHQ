In this building you mainly find:

THOMAS VAN AQUINOSTRAAT - VISUAL APPEARANCE / WHAT TO LOOK FOR
The Thomas van Aquinostraat buildings form a row of older faculty blocks with a distinct street-like profile.
Typical visual cues:
- Height: a row of low- to mid-rise education blocks, forming a linear sequence along the street.
- Materials: dark brick facades with concrete lintels and replacement window systems from different renovation phases.
- Colours: dark brick with lighter window bands; a visible patina from age and upgrades.
- Shapes / layout: repetitive facades along a straight academic street; rectangular blocks with clear entrances and stair cores.

From the outside, Thomas van Aquinostraat 1 reads as one of these classic brick faculty blocks: linear, rhythmic facades with a slightly older feel than newer buildings like EOS or MM, but clearly still in active educational use.

THOMAS VAN AQUINOSTRAAT - ENVIRONMENT AROUND THE BUILDING
- Greenery: a tree row and grassy verges along the street, giving it a somewhat quieter, green corridor atmosphere.
- Bikes: bike racks along the street edge; students often park directly in front of the entrances.
- Traffic / vibe: mostly quiet, with small groups arriving for seminars, honours sessions, or meetings; less of a large-lecture rush than Collegezalencomplex or Comenius.

THOMAS VAN AQUINOSTRAAT - HISTORY / FORMER ASSOCIATION USE
Historically, the Thomas van Aquinostraat zone functioned more strongly as an association and student-organisation corridor, with several student-oriented offices and spaces located in its blocks. Over time, many of these association functions have been relocated or phased out, and Thomas van Aquinostraat 1 is no longer treated as a dedicated association hub. Students may still encounter central university services and organisation offices here, but the building is now primarily framed as a teaching and support location rather than a classic ???association building???.
